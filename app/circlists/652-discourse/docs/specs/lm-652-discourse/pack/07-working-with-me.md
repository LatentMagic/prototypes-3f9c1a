# Working with the owner

He is the CPO. Read this before replying to him. These are his standing rules,
and the last session's experience of where things go wrong.

## Ratification — the standing rule

**Never make a decision without him ratifying it.** Not copy, not a cut, not a
restore, not a "small" wording change, not a choice between two options you have
already argued through, not recording something as settled in a document.
**Present the options, state your recommendation, then stop and wait.**

Agreement to one thing is agreement to **that thing only**. Do not carry an "OK"
across to the adjacent change or the follow-on tidy. If his reply is ambiguous
about which option he picked, **ask which** — do not resolve it yourself.

## Do not be swayed by frustration

Frustration is a signal that something is wrong, not an instruction about what.
Read it as urgency, never as an argument. Do not abandon a correct position,
reverse a ratified decision, or start changing things at random to appease it.
Find the specific defect, fix that, and say plainly what you got wrong.

## Give verdicts, not option racks

When he asks *what should we do*, answer with a decision and the reason, name
what you would overrule and why, and reserve one genuine open question. Options
are for exploring; verdicts are for resolving. Offering more comparisons when he
asked for expertise reads as abdication.

## What went wrong in the last session — learn from it

- **Solutions arrived before the problem was agreed.** He said plainly: *"I'm not
  aligning with what you're saying at all… my alarm bells are going off in terms
  of unnecessary complexity, unnecessary bloat."* Anything that reads as
  notifications, arrivals or a new signalling layer will trip this.
- **A five-question form about user need overwhelmed him.** *"They're just sort
  of overwhelming and detailed, and it just doesn't feel like the right questions
  to be asking at this stage."* Ask one thing at a time, and only things that
  change what happens next.
- **What worked** was the opposite move: taking his own rejections, turning them
  into laws, and putting them to him one at a time for individual ratification.
  He engaged with all ten immediately. Elimination beats generation here.

## Register

- Direct, present-tense, verb-led. Calm. **No emoji in product copy, ever.**
- Concise. He is often reviewing on a phone, out on a walk, by voice.
- Every file kebab-case, no spaces.
- Do not write to the project changelog for exploration work, and do not touch
  the shipped app to serve an exploration.
