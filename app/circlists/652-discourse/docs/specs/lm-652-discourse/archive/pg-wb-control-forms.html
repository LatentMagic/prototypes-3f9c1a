Ten forms for the talking control

Two places survived and both were judged on a single guess at the object. So the object is the variable here: five different forms for a control that belongs to the circle’s name, and five for one that lives in the settings corner. Not five fillings of the same box — different shapes, different anchors, different amounts of the bar consumed. The web bars fill the page, standing in for the width the bar gets beside the 240px rail, and the name is long enough to show what each form costs it. The app bar is drawn at 390px for the name family; the settings family has no host in the app bar at all, which is its standing cost.

Family N · it belongs to the nameThe leading end of the bar. Everything here is either inside the name slot or in the empty space before it.
N1A second line under the name
The name slot becomes two lines: the name, and beneath it the sage rule with a short phrase. Nothing is added horizontally; the whole slot is the target. This is B5 as first drawn, kept so the rest have something to beat.Costs: The bar stops being a fixed frame for one line of type, the name shifts up whenever something starts talking, and the phrase sits so close to the name that the two read as one wrapped title.

how to read them

On the desktop bar none of the ten costs the name a character — there is room for all of them. The name family is decided at 390px, where the name wants more room than the bar has: every form there truncates it, so the question is not whether but how much, and whether what is lost still identifies the circle.

The settings family is not decided by width at all. It is decided by what the corner claims: whether the count and circle settings are one object, two, or one plus an ornament — and what happens to the corner’s shape on the ordinary day when nothing is talking.
