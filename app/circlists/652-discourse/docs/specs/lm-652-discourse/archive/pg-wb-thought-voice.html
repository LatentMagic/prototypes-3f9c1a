LM-652 · whiteboard — the thought's voice

One question: what makes the contributed thought read as Priya, speaking to the circle — and not as a pull-quote, an extract or a subheading belonging to the article. The trap is that every conventional way of making text look special (quote marks, serif, italic, indent, left rule) is the visual language of citation, so it pushes the words toward the article.

Same thought, same card, four treatments, each shown at all three scales it has to survive. Nothing here is wired, and the domain question is deliberately untouched — the open card is exactly as it stands today.

1Run on
Signal: grammar. The name does not sit apart from the words — it begins them, and the thought runs on from it as one unbroken sentence of speech. Nothing is framed, so there is nothing to mistake for a frame. Cost: at paragraph length the name is far behind by the time you finish, and the foot row then carries a second attribution.

2The hand
Signal: the face itself. The thought is the one thing on the screen set in the mono face. It is typed, not typeset — and no article was ever set this way, so it cannot be read as matter lifted from one. Cost: mono already means raw address here (the URL fallback headline, the badges), and a long thought in mono is tiring to read.

3Said so
Signal: language. A quiet line above the words names the act — Priya added this and said. A sentence tells you what you are about to read, so no typographic move has to, and the type can stay exactly the card's. Cost: a line of copy on every card that carries a thought, and at band scale it competes with the words for the one line there is.

4The paper
Signal: substrate. The type does not change at all — the paper under it does. The thought runs full-bleed to the card's inner edges on the warm stock the writing field already uses, so it reads as a surface someone wrote on rather than a block someone quoted. Cost: a second colour of paper inside a card, on a shelf of cards. This is the treatment already judged badly once as an inset block; full-bleed with no border is a different animal, but it may still be one box too many.
