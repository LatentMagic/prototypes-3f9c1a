What goes inside the button

Two places survived: after the circle’s name (B5’s slot, as a box rather than a second line) and joined to circle settings as one object (B2). Both were judged on a single guess at what the control contains, so here the content is the variable and the two places are held constant — same five contents, drawn in both, and again in the app bar at 390px where the name has least room. The name is deliberately long in every cell: whatever the control holds, the name pays for it.

C1The count alone
The sage rule and a numeral. Nothing is named, nothing is claimed — the smallest thing that can carry a quantity.Costs: A bare number in a box does not say what it counts. Read cold, on the way in, it could be members, unread, anything.

what the drawings settle

Width is the whole argument. In the B5 slot the control sits between the name and the space before settings, so every character it holds is taken from the name — at 390px C2, C3 and C5 truncate the name outright, while C1 and C4 leave it intact. In the B2 slot the pair is anchored to the right edge, so the name keeps its full run and the cost moves to the object’s bulk instead.

Joined to settings, the content also has to survive sitting next to the word “Circle settings”: C4 reads as an ornament on the gear, and C3’s faces read as the members of the circle rather than the people who spoke.
