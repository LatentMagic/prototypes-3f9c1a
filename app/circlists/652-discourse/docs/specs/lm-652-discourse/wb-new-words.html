New words in a conversation

The wash currently paints the whole turn. A turn is a flexible block of words, so the shape it fills is a padded rectangle around a paragraph — arbitrary at every length, and a stack of them reads as slabs. The green also carries the accent's action meaning, which nothing here is asking you to do. Three ways out, each moving the mark to a different scale: the conversation, the gutter, the byline. Every frame is seeded the same way — a run of new turns at the foot AND one new reply buried in an older group, which is the case each option has to survive.

00What is there now

Stance — the wash from the feed's arrival glow, held as a resting state under the whole turn.

Cost — the fill has no shape of its own, competes with the accent, and multiplies down a thread.

01The waterline

Stance — newness is a position in the conversation, not a property of each turn. One rule where you left off, and it tells you where to start reading rather than what to look at.

Cost — it only marks a contiguous run at the foot. The new reply inside the older group sits above the line and gets nothing, so a conversation that grows in its middle is unserved.

02The margin mark

Stance — the return banner's own tab, at turn scale: sage, 3×22, radius 2, standing in the row's flow at the row's gap. Not a new mark — the one the product already uses to say this is the thing that came back. The tab is always in the geometry, so nothing shifts when it colours.

Cost — it lands in the same gutter the reply rail occupies, so a new reply shows two vertical lines within 13px. It is also markedly quieter than the wash, which is the point and the risk.

03The unread byline

Stance — no ground and no gutter. A dot after the name, and the bylines of turns you have read recede; the same mark the rail already uses for a circle with something in it, at the scale of one turn.

Cost — the weakest of the three at a glance, and it dims what you have already read to make the new stand out, which is a claim about old words the product has not made anywhere else.

Three new

For context, not options — how the three patterns behave elsewhere

The labelled divider

Group chat draws one line across the transcript at the last-read point, labelled, and pins it until you leave. Position, not emphasis.

The margin dot

Mail lists put a filled dot in the left margin of each unread row, and clear it on read. Per item, and countable down the column.

The tinted row

Forums and trackers tint the whole unread row. Loud and unmissable at list scale, where every row is the same height.
