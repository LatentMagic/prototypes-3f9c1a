The edit box's way out

An edit box is the one box in this product that needs a way out: clearing the field does not give the original words back. The arrow is the only glyph here that means commit, so the way out cannot be a second one — the question is where it stands. Every frame is the same turn of yours mid-edit; the right-hand frame is the same box near the 500 cap, where the remaining-count shares the field's edge with whatever is put there.

01Beside the arrow — as built

Stance — one slot on the field's edge holds both: the way out unfilled and unbordered, the commit the only inked box, the arrow in the exact corner it holds in every other field.

Cost — two controls in a row read as a pair of choices, and the ✗ sits where the eye expects nothing. It eats 78px of the text's right gutter, and pushes the count further in.

02Stacked above the arrow

Stance — the placement chat apps use: the two acts share one column at the field's right edge, so the gutter stays one control wide and the commit keeps the bottom corner.

Cost — the field can never be shorter than two controls, so a one-line edit stands ~104px tall. The count now runs under the stack rather than beside it.

03Out of the field, onto the box

Stance — commit and abandon are not siblings. The arrow keeps the field's edge alone, exactly as everywhere else, and the way out sits at the top right of the words being edited — where the ⋯ that opened the edit stood.

Cost — the two acts are far apart, and the ✗ lands in the name row, which so far has only ever carried the menu. On a reply, the row is tighter.

04The word, not the mark

Stance — no new glyph enters the product at all. A quiet word on the field's edge says what pressing it does, which no ✗ can; discarding words is worth one plain reading.

Cost — it is the loudest option and the widest, taking 112px of gutter, and it re-introduces a text control beside the field after the buttons beneath it were taken out.
