# Discourse — ideation on your notes, then a new steering

Input: Jonny's six reactions and your nine, from the v1 playground
(`discourse-playground.html`). Nothing here has been applied to the app. The
playground built alongside this doc is `discourse-playground-v2.html`.

**How to read it.** Part 1 takes every note one at a time and pushes it as far as
it goes on its own. Part 2 is the ideation that had never happened —
continuation. Part 3 is what all of it adds up to when the individual answers are
put in one room, and Part 4 is what v2 therefore is. Part 5 is what I still need
from you.

The single most useful thing that came out of taking the notes separately: **most
of them are not votes between the eight directions. They are statements about
three different axes that v1 had welded together.** Six of the eight "directions"
turn out to be levers or rules, and the one question that actually needs
directions — continuation — had no directions at all.

## Part 1 — Every note, taken one at a time

### 1. Jonny: wants the poster's thoughts *before* reading. You: same — explore the contributor's thought up front (the contributor one, not the conversation)

You both landed on this independently, and it is the note that reshapes
everything else.

**What you were reaching for.** The note is not commentary on the link — it *is*
the recommendation. It is the reason to pick this item out of eight. Hiding it
until after the read means the queue is a wall of headlines with nothing to
choose between, and the one piece of information that would make you choose is
sealed behind the act of choosing. That is backwards.

**The extrapolation.** Reveal-on-read was written as one rule, but it is
protecting two different things, and only one of them needs protecting:

- **The invitation** — what the sharer says to get you to look. Someone handing
  you a book says why. They do not hand you a sealed envelope. This is *part of
  the share*, the same class of information as the title.
- **The conversation** — what other members said once they had been through it.
  This is what would spoil, prime, and pressure you. This is what reveal-on-read
  exists for.

So the rule sharpens to something better than it was:

> **Reveal-on-read protects the conversation, not the invitation.**

That keeps every reason reveal-on-read was introduced (no spoilers from readers,
no performance in the feed, no reading the reactions instead of the thing) and
gives up nothing, because the sharer cannot spoil a piece they are recommending
*to* you — they are pitching it, not reviewing it.

**Where it does bite, and the answer.** Sometimes the honest pitch *is* a spoiler
("the ending caught me off guard"). Rather than solve that with a rule, give it
to the person who knows: **the sharer can seal their own line** — one control on
the Add sheet, "keep this until they've read it". Visible by default, sealed by
choice. The person who wrote the sentence is the only one who can tell whether it
gives anything away.

**Second consequence, less obvious.** A line that is visible before the read must
be short enough to sit on a card in a list without turning the list into a blog.
The constraint belongs at the *input*, not the display — never truncate somebody's
sentence with an ellipsis. So a visible preface implies a real character ceiling
(90–140), enforced in the composer.

**Verdict: a rule, not an option.** Prefaces on the card, always, on both tabs.

### 2. Jonny: guided statements — on the fence. Specialising, holds your hand; also risks being opinionated, presumptuous, belittling

Both halves of that are correct, which is the clue: **the scaffold and the
authorship are separable, and only the authorship is the problem.**

The scaffold does real work — it kills the blank-box stare, and it enforces
brevity by grammar rather than by a rule. The authorship is what stings: the app
put words in your mouth, and the *record* then shows the app's words in bold with
yours trailing after, so the circle reads like a form somebody filled in.

**The extrapolation — three strengths of the same idea:**

- **A prompt in the placeholder.** Rotating, unemphatic, gone the moment you
  type. All of the hand-holding, none of the residue. Nothing of the app's
  survives into the record.
- **A tappable prefill.** Tap "Shared because" and it becomes *your* text, fully
  editable, deletable to the last character. A starting point, not a frame.
- **A grammar** (v1). The stem is structural and renders as a label on the card
  forever. This is the version that reads as a form.

The first two are helpful; the third is the one that belittles. And note what
happens to the whole "direction": once the stem stops being structural, guided
statements is not an answer to *what shape does discourse take* at all. It is an
answer to *how do we help you write*.

**Verdict: a lever on the composer, not a direction.** Default to the placeholder
prompt; keep the prefill as a position; keep the v1 grammar as the third position
so it can be seen losing.

### 3. Jonny: the Table — not into the detachment. You: interesting but a problem — it separates things too much, and the point wasn't fully explored: a card with enough exploration could be separated, and the major point is being able to *continue*

Your correction rescues it, and it is the most productive note in the set.

**What was wrong.** v1 graduated a card to the Table the moment anybody said
anything. So everything graduated, the queue split for no reason, and discourse
started competing with reading. That is the detachment Jonny felt: content was
*taken away* from where it lived.

**What you were actually describing.** The Table is not "the place discourse
lives". It is the place for the **rare item that stopped being a link and became a
conversation** — and its whole justification is that it is the only place where a
conversation may *continue*. Three things follow:

- **A threshold, and a real one.** Graduation has to be earned: either a member
  deliberately takes it there, or a genuine floor of exchange is crossed (three
  voices). A member's act is more Circlists — nothing detected, nothing
  triggered, a person decides — and it means the Table is never a surprise.
- **The card does not leave.** Graduating must not move anything. The item stays
  exactly where it was in Active/Read and gains one quiet line saying it is at
  the table. **The Table is a lens over the same library, not a second library.**
  That single change removes the detachment entirely.
- **It has to drain.** A room that only fills is a room you stop entering. Which
  is what makes Part 2's "land it" necessary rather than decorative.

**Verdict: keep the Table, but only as the room where continuation is legal.**
Without continuation it has no reason to exist — you were right that on its own
it is actively harmful.

### 4. Jonny: the Echo feels complementary to everything else, and very interesting — explore further. You: I didn't understand it; I need more examples

Taking those together: the idea is strong and the *presentation* of it failed.

**Why it feels complementary.** Because it is not a direction — it is a **verb**.
Every other direction answers "where does discourse live"; echo answers "what is
the cheapest possible thing you can say", and that answer is compatible with all
of them.

**What it actually is, said plainly.** A glyph reacts to *the link*. An echo
reacts to *a person's sentence*. That is the whole difference, and it is the
difference between a like-button and being answered: an echo is **addressed**.
"Priya said the same about your note" is a belonging signal a reaction cannot
carry, because reactions are about the thing, not about you.

**Three extrapolations that make it earn Jonny's interest:**

- **It is the participation floor.** One tap, no composition, no fear of writing
  the wrong thing. Which means the quiet member is not silent — and the empty
  slot with your name on it (v1's stated cost) stops being empty by default.
- **It renders as presence, never as a count.** Faces tucked under the sentence
  they agreed with. You can count them if you want; nothing is displaying a
  number. Same discipline as the disc, which shows a huddle of glyphs and never
  "6 reactions".
- **It is the legal way to respond to a response — and it keeps depth at one.**
  This is the elegant bit. The rule "no threads" was doing a lot of damage: you
  could not react to what anybody said. But an echo *cannot itself be echoed*.
  It is terminal by construction. So: **you may respond to a response, but only
  by pointing.** Depth stops at one without a rule that says depth stops at one.

**On the word.** "Echo" is why you did not get it — it is a metaphor you have to
decode before you can use it. The plain version of the gesture is *same*: "Priya,
Dev and Lena said the same." Instantly legible, right register — warm, low-cost,
not a like. v2 defaults to **Same** and keeps Echo as a lever so you can hear
both. And it is shown in four places rather than described once: on a preface, on
a response, in the record, and from the moment of reading.

**Verdict: a primitive available on every line, in every direction.**

### 5. Jonny: loves reactions and reflections together, particularly on Read. Slightly harder on contribution — that may need to be a separate moment for interactivity. A beautiful synergy. You: don't like how the reveal hangs waiting for the responses; that timeout doesn't work in that context. Separate sections may have value; I also quite like it all coming together — but there's a spacing thing, because you immediately get into a scroll

These are the same subject from both ends — **display** and **input** — and they
resolve in opposite directions, which is exactly what Jonny's "slightly harder on
contribution" is sensing.

**Display: merge them, completely.** The record of an item is not "reactions, and
separately some words". How it landed on Priya *is* her glyph, her depth, and her
sentence. So the roster under the disc stops being a list of names and becomes
**the record**: each member's row carries their glyph and, when they left one,
their line. The disc is the *shape* of how it landed; the list underneath is the
*substance*. One artefact, one surface, one moment. Nothing new is invented — the
door's roster simply grows the thing it was always missing.

**Input: two beats, one breath.** Reacting is a *gesture* — physical, quick,
pointer-driven. Writing is a *composition*. Overlapping them makes both worse
(a textarea appearing mid-drag kills the gesture; a drag pad above a textarea
makes the writing feel like a form field). So keep two beats — but never let the
container leave. The reaction commits and the *same sheet* becomes the record,
with your line at the bottom of it. Two beats, one breath.

**And that kills the timeout, which is the actual bug you hit.** Today the shipped
reveal is a passive five-second glimpse that dismisses itself. It was designed
for a small payload — a disc. Put words in it and the five seconds become dead
air *while you wait to be shown something substantial*, and then the sheet leaves
on its own schedule and a second sheet slides up. Two dismissals, one hang, for
one action.

> **The reveal stops being a timed glimpse and becomes the record.** It opens
> when you commit, it holds what the circle said, it carries your composer, and
> **you** dismiss it. No timer anywhere.

Nothing is lost — the drama the five seconds was buying moves into the content
arriving: the disc paints, then the sentences land in sequence over about 400ms.
A reveal that *reads* as revealing and never blocks. The beat becomes an
entrance rather than a wait.

**On the spacing / immediate scroll.** Right — and it is a symptom, not a
spacing bug. The moment-of-arrival surface was carrying a 216px disc, a roster,
a preface, every response, and a composer. Two structural fixes rather than
tighter padding:

- **The moment gets a compact disc, the door gets the full one.** At the moment of
  reading you need to know *that* it landed and *what* was said; the full shape is
  what the door is for, one tap away. So the moment fits without scrolling on a
  390-wide phone.
- **Others' words are bounded at the moment, complete in the door.** Two lines
  then a quiet "3 more" — the progressive disclosure the card stack already uses.

And because you said you like it coming together *and* that separate sections may
have value, that is a lever, not a decision: **Merged** (each row carries its
words) or **Sectioned** (how it landed, a rule, then what was said).

**Verdict: merge on display; two beats in one container on input; delete the
timer.**

### 6. Jonny: the contributor is more prominent on Question, and that's nice — higher priority because of the ownership, and because it's a reply

This reads as a note about type, and it is really a note about **structure**.

The question option happened to set the sharer's line a size up and semibold,
and everything else became visibly subordinate to it. That felt right because it
*is* right: the exchange is not a flat list of peers. It has a head — the
sharer's line, which they own and which everything else is answering — and a body
of replies to it.

**The extrapolation.** State it as a rule and apply it everywhere, not only to
questions:

> **The preface outranks the responses. Always a size up, always full weight;
> responses set smaller and lighter, indented under it.**

That does something the "no threads" rule could not: it makes the *shape* of a
legal exchange visible. One head, N replies, and no reply can have a head of its
own. The constraint is expressed as hierarchy rather than as a prohibition, which
is why the question option felt structurally sound rather than merely well set.

**Separately: the question register itself is worth keeping** — but as a choice
the *sharer* makes on the Add sheet, not as a whole direction. A real circle has
both: "here's why" and "here's what I want to know". The register changes what
the response is *for* (an answer, not a reflection), and the card can then offer
"answer" rather than "say something".

**Verdict: a typographic rule everywhere, plus a register the sharer picks.**

### 7. You: don't like Read having questions bloating the card — they have to be hidden, and the one we really like works really well

Read next to note 1, this is a clean split rather than a contradiction, and it
settles the whole information architecture:

> **The sharer's line belongs on the card. Everything the circle said belongs
> behind the door.**

The card therefore never grows with the conversation. It grows by exactly one
line — the invitation — and that line is the same on Active and on Read. The Read
card differs from the Active card only in that the tick is a door.

**One thing the door then needs.** If the record holds words and the card shows
no trace, nobody opens it. But a count is forbidden and a badge is forbidden. The
app already has an answer to "there is something in here you have not seen": the
**micro dot** from the liveliness grammar. So the door carries a micro dot when
the record holds words you have not seen, and drops it once you have. No new
vocabulary, no numbers, and it composes with a signal the product already
teaches.

### 8. You: do like that if you skip, you can fix that on Read

Small note, load-bearing principle:

> **Every moment is re-enterable. Nothing expires.**

You skipped the reaction, or read it and had nothing to say, or had something to
say three days later. The door is always open and always offers you your line if
you have not left one. This is the calm floor doing real work: no missed
opportunity, no closing window, nothing that punishes the slow reader. It is also
why "one response per member" is survivable at all — the one response is not a
one-time chance.

(It raises, and I have not answered: can you *change* your line after you leave
it? See Part 2's "living line" and Part 5.)

### 9. You: inside the door is sick, could be a good grounding. Card flip is very cool, but liked less

"Grounding" is the right word for it, and note 7 is why: the door is the only
place that can hold the whole record without any of it leaking onto a card. It
already exists, it is already the app's way of asking "how did this land", and
its boundary is already understood. Discourse does not need a new home; it needs
the home it has to hold more.

The flip is a lovely piece of craft that answers a question nobody has: it puts
the record one gesture away instead of one tap away, and pays for it with a
surface that cannot hold much, cannot scroll comfortably, and has no obvious way
back. **Parked, not built.** The code is in the v1 modules if it is ever wanted.

## Part 2 — Continuation: the ideation that had never happened

Your biggest note, and correctly identified as a gap: v1 asked *what shape does
an exchange take* and then only ever allowed one turn each, so it never had to
answer **what happens when one line is not enough**.

The trap is obvious and worth naming: the easy answer is a thread, and a thread
is a chat, and a chat is a product we have said three times over we are not
building. But "no threads" was never the goal — the goal is that the circle's
library does not turn into an inbox you owe replies to.

So the question to ideate on is not "threads: yes or no". It is:

> **What is the discipline that lets a second turn happen without the library
> becoming a feed you have to keep up with?**

Seven answers. Each is a different *theory* of where the discipline lives, and
each is a real option in the v2 playground.

### K0 — One line each, and that is all *(the floor)*
The discipline is prohibition. You get one line, forever.
- **Why it is not a chat:** there is nothing to reply to.
- **Cost:** a question cannot be answered back. The record is a set of parallel
  statements that never touch. This is the gap you named.

### K1 — Your line is living *(change it, never add to it)*
You have exactly one line per item forever — and you can rewrite it whenever you
like. Coming back a week later and changing your mind *is* the continuation. The
record never grows; it ripens.
- **Why it is not a chat:** growth is impossible by construction. The artefact is
  always one glance, forever.
- **Novel bit:** a line is yours until somebody touches it — once another member
  has echoed or answered it, it locks. It stops being your sentence and becomes
  part of the circle's record.
- **Cost:** history vanishes. Somebody may be answering a sentence that no longer
  exists, and the record reads as settled consensus rather than as an exchange.

### K2 — One line each, plus one to each person *(breadth, capped)*
Your budget: one line about the item, and one line to each member who spoke. A
reply is **addressed** — it renders beside the line it answers, marked *to Priya*,
never nested underneath.
- **Why it is not a chat:** depth is 1 by construction, and breadth is capped by
  the size of the circle. The worst case is knowable, which a thread's never is.
- **Novel bit:** the record is a small matrix of who answered whom, not a tree.
  And because a reply is to a *person* rather than to a room, it reads as
  answering someone instead of posting.
- **Cost:** in a full circle that is a lot of lines. Bounded is not the same as
  small.

### K3 — Continuation needs a room *(the Table — your correction, built)*
In the feed and the door, one line each. When an item has real exchange, any
member may **take it to the table** — and only there can you speak more than
once. The Table shows the same items, never copies. It reads as a page, not a
message list. And any member can **land it** with a closing takeaway, which
freezes the record and drains the item from the room.
- **Why it is not a chat:** you have to walk somewhere to have a conversation, and
  the room holds two items, not two hundred.
- **Novel bit:** the exit. A conversation that must end is a conversation that
  stays short, and "what we took from this" is the natural end of a reading loop
  — the Swell closes the read; landing closes the exchange. Same family of
  gesture, same vocabulary: things *land* here.
- **Cost:** a place you enter is a place you can forget to enter, and a quiet
  circle may never fill it.

### K4 — Continue by asking *(every turn after your first must be a question)*
Speak as often as you like. But your first turn is a statement and every turn
after it must be a question.
- **Why it is not a chat:** a question cannot be a monologue, and it always hands
  the floor to somebody else. Continuation is structurally generous.
- **Novel bit:** it makes the record read as an enquiry, which is what a reading
  circle actually is — and it is the natural extension of the register Jonny
  liked in note 6.
- **Cost:** it is a grammar, so it inherits note 2's whole risk. Sometimes you
  just want to agree, and being told to phrase agreement as a question is exactly
  the belittling failure.

### K5 — Rounds, closed by reading *(not by a clock)*
Discourse happens in rounds. Everyone who has read the item may leave one line;
the round closes when everyone who has read it has spoken, and a new one opens.
- **Why it is not a chat:** length is replaced by structure. The record has
  movements rather than a tail.
- **Novel bit:** the trigger is *reading*, never time — so there is no deadline,
  no "closes in 2 days", nothing that can make you late. Rounds are the one
  time-like structure that survives the calm floor.
- **Cost:** one member who never speaks freezes the round forever, and "waiting
  for the round" is a quiet form of waiting for people.

### K6 — Land it, and until then say anything *(discipline at the exit)*
No limit on turns at all. What stops the sprawl is that the record's **head** is
a takeaway somebody wrote, and once landed the item takes no new lines.
- **Why it is not a chat:** the ration is on the open state, not on the talking. A
  conversation that must end stays short without anybody being rationed.
- **Novel bit:** the artefact's final form is a *conclusion* rather than a
  transcript — the most useful thing the circle produced, at the top, forever.
- **Cost:** the middle can sprawl before anyone lands it, and closing an exchange
  is a power one member holds over the others.

### What is common to all of them, and worth saying out loud

Every one of these avoids being Slack by a *different* mechanism, and none of
them needs the visual grammar of a chat. Which points at a rule that costs
nothing and does an enormous amount of work:

> **A continued exchange is set as a page, not as a transcript.** Attribution in
> the margin, paragraphs in the column. No bubbles, no left/right alignment, no
> per-line timestamps, no avatar column with a tail. The typography of an
> annotated document.

Whatever we choose in Part 2, that is how it should look.

## Part 3 — What all of it adds up to

Putting the individual answers in one room, they do not argue. They compose into
one shape — which is why v2 leads with a spine instead of eight rival directions.

1. **Reveal-on-read protects the conversation, not the invitation.** *(1, 7)*
2. **The sharer's line lives on the card, on both tabs, and can be sealed by its
   author.** *(1, 7)*
3. **Everything the circle said lives behind the door — the card never grows with
   the conversation.** *(7, 9)*
4. **The record is reactions and words in one artefact:** the disc is the shape,
   the roster is the substance. *(5)*
5. **The reveal is the record, opened at the moment you commit and dismissed by
   you. No timer.** *(5)*
6. **The preface outranks the responses**, everywhere, typographically. *(6)*
7. **Same/echo is a verb available on every line** — the participation floor, and
   the only legal way to answer a response. *(4)*
8. **Continuation is real, and its discipline is the open question.** *(K0–K6)*
9. **Every moment is re-enterable. Nothing expires.** *(8)*
10. **A continued exchange is set as a page, not a transcript.** *(Part 2)*

Guided statements, the flip, marginalia and "passing notes" do not appear as
directions here because none of them survived being taken seriously as an answer
to a question: two are levers, one is parked, and two were the same idea as the
spine with the preface hidden.

## Part 4 — So v2 is

**One settled spine, and the open question given real options.**

- **Pane 1 — The shape.** The ten statements above, each traceable to the note it
  came from, so you can see what was heard and argue with any of it.
- **Pane 2 — Continuation.** K0–K6 as seven selectable directions, each with its
  claim, its cost, and its own theory of why it is not a chat. This is where the
  decision is.
- **Pane 3 — Levers.** Everything the notes turned into a lever: preface
  visibility, prompt style, the echo word, merged vs sectioned record, the read
  card's density, graduation threshold, landing, names, length — plus the state
  switches.
- **Pane 4 — The loop**, now six beats: *shared with a reason → it waits →
  you finish it → the record opens in the same breath → it continues → it lands.*

Built on the real app, as before: the shipped reaction pad is the real component,
the door is the app's door, no bezel unless you force the posture, and the config
rail is the circle rail.

**Parked deliberately:** the card flip; marginalia as its own direction; stems as
a grammar (kept only as the losing position on a lever); the Table as a
destination that takes content out of the feed.

## Part 5 — What I need from you

1. **Continuation is the decision.** K0–K6. My own read: **K3 with K6's ending**
   is the strongest — continuation exists, it is bounded by place, and it has a
   real end — and **K2** is the most surprising, because addressed-but-flat gives
   genuine back-and-forth for a cost you can calculate. K4 is the one I would
   most like to be wrong about, because it is beautiful and probably too clever.
2. **Sealing your own preface** — is one control on the Add sheet worth it, or is
   "visible, always, keep it short" the calmer rule even though it can spoil?
3. **Can you change your line?** K1 makes it the whole mechanic; everywhere else
   it is a small question with a sharp edge (someone answered the old words).
4. **"Same" or "Echo"?** Same is legible on sight; Echo is ours. Flip it in the
   levers pane and see which one you want on a card.
5. **Does the micro dot on the door read as arrival, or as unread-count-by-other-
   means?** It is the one place I have added a signal rather than removed one.
6. **The question register** — sharer-chosen on the Add sheet, or not a thing?
