The bold row

One rule holds every frame below, and it is what makes weight worth taking seriously: a circle's name is bold exactly when that circle's return bar is standing. Not a second signal with its own lifecycle — the same state the bar already holds, rendered a second time in the rail. It appears when someone speaks on a card you watch, it survives tapping in and leaving, and it goes when you have opened the last card the bar was holding. Five readings of that rule, then the ten-circle test, then the red team.

Seeding is unchanged from the last board: Long reads has talk on a card you watch, The Fifth Floor has new links, Tuesday Club has both — and in the third frame you have been to all three, opening Long reads' card and one of Tuesday's two.

The synergy question — one state, rendered twice

The rail says which circle; the bar inside it says which cards; the card's own margin tab says which turns. Bold is attractive precisely because it defines nothing new: it is the bar's existence, projected onto the row. Where it strains is the circle you are already in — the selected row is bold for being selected, so it cannot also be bold for owing you a reply. In that one case the bar is the only carrier, which is acceptable, because you are looking at the bar.

Red team — the case against the bold row

Weight is spoken for. The rail has exactly one weight change today and it means you are here. Adding a second meaning to the same variable is the kind of overloading that took the micro dot off the table in the first place.

It borrows a grammar without its system. Bold-for-unread works in team chat because it sits beside mute, mentions, badges and a channel list built to be scanned for unreads. Here it arrives alone, in a rail built to be chosen from.

Habituation cuts the other way. A dot is an event and reads as one. A bold name is a property, and a circle that is bold for nine days reads as a circle that is simply called that — the signal becomes the name.

Figure and ground invert with volume. At six of ten bold, the eye starts reading the four quiet rows as the exception. The signal has not got louder, it has changed which set it describes.

It cannot be seen by everyone. Weight alone is a thin cue at 14px for low vision and invisible to a screen reader without added text — the dot ships with a hidden ", new items" and bold would need its own, which means the rule must be stated in words anyway.

It answers a question nobody asked from the rail. The rail is where you choose where to go. Bold tells you where work is, and quietly turns a shelf of places into a queue — which is the shape the product has refused everywhere else.
