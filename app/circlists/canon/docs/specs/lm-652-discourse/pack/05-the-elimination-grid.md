are omitted — their option models are dead and their mechanisms recur here. The
column names the **first** law broken, not all of them.

## What the grid did — reading, not ratified

**It killed mechanisms, not ideas.** Every kill is unboundedness, deferral,
exclusion or destruction. In three cases that is incidental to the idea:

- **The Record** — the direction he responded to most warmly — dies on L4, on
  exactly the defect he named. Bound the record and the mechanism is intact.
- **The Dispatch** dies on cadence, which is its delivery, not its instinct. The
  problem it attacks is real and is stated below.
- **The Stream** dies on being unfinishable, which is a property of a river, not
  of a talk surface.

**What survives is one family, not fifteen options.** Strip the presentation off
and the survivors all say the same thing:

> One bounded utterance per member per item, set down in a place that already
> exists, revealed under the rules already settled.

They differ only in *where it is set down* (card, margin, door, disc, a place)
and *what form it takes* (a note, a pull, a point, a proposition, a stem).

## The live material from the review

Four things the owner responded to. Three are statements of a problem, not
answers:

- **Replying to someone while still leaving your own line** (from The Record) —
  *"that's actually really interesting"*. A mechanic, and the only one.
- **You do not know that readers 3 and 4 have arrived** (from The Dispatch) — a
  problem.
- **The cascade** (from Palimpsest) — reading something fourth is genuinely not
  the same as reading it first, because more of the circle is now in it. A
  problem, and an interesting one.
- **Return** — a card sinks down the Read tab; there is no sort, no filter, no
  pin. Every direction assumed you would find your way back and none earned it.
  Agreed in substance to sit **above** continuation as the fundamental tension.
