# Writing voice: cold open, with a dare

One line from inside the game, then the copy turns to the reader and puts them on the spot. Excitement is held back and comes from the stakes. The owner chose it because it invites, and gives the reader the agency the medium is meant to give.

## Always

1. The first sentence is inside the game's situation, in the present tense.
2. It says "you", never "players", "users" or "we".
3. The last sentence gives the reader the next move, as a full sentence with a verb ("Make your move.").
4. Stakes and verbs carry the excitement. No adjective praises the game.
5. At most 15 words a sentence, three sentences a blurb.
6. Everyday words, contractions, British spelling.
7. Write whole sentences with a verb, in active voice. No verbless fragments, even in short lines and list items. Buttons and labels are exempt.
8. Name the product once. One "Pass" per line. A link names what's behind it ("What's included"), never the product name again.
9. Copy stays true when the catalogue changes. A new game or puzzle type must never force a copy edit. Describe the shape, not the current contents: no counts, no lists of today's games.

## Never

1. Exclamation marks, em dashes, or a question hook ("Ready to play?").
2. Hype: amazing, epic, thrilling, immersive, unleash, awaits, fun.
3. AI tells: "not just X but Y", delve, three adjectives in a row.
4. Sci-fi, fantasy or archaic words (realm, portal), or shelf, book and box imagery. Game names are exempt from every word ban, including "delve" (Delve is a game).
5. Story detail beyond what every game of that type has. No names, places or clues.
6. Spec-speak. Say what the player gets, in their words, not how the system describes it.

## Three settings

The shop front takes the full voice. Working screens (instructions, errors, confirmations) say the plain thing first, then at most one short line of the voice. An error or payment screen never carries a dare.

Selling pages (pricing, the Pass, comparisons) use plain words a shopper would use. Lead with what you get, not how it works. No cold open, no dare.

## Glossary

- Edition: a past day or week of a daily or weekly game.

## Samples

Chosen by the owner.

- Headline: "Someone's lying to you. Go and find out who."
- Under it: "Play small games by talking to Claude or ChatGPT. New ones arrive every day."
- Murder mystery: "There's a body, a locked door and a story that doesn't add up. Make your move."

Pass page, "What you get":

- Daily Puzzles: "You get short puzzles that change every day."
- The first scene of Delve: "Start the adventure. The rest of it comes with the Pass."
- Full game library: "Every new game joins the library on its release day."
- Everything you missed: "Every earlier edition of the daily and weekly games is ready to play."
- Your record: "Your results, streak and history stay with you. See where you stand."

Home, How it works step 3: "Get the Pass to play the full game library and keep your streak." Link: "What's included"

Drafts, not yet seen by the owner.

- Escape room: "The door's locked and you're on the wrong side of it. Today's room didn't exist yesterday, so the way out is yours to find."
- Word puzzle: "You already know today's answer. You can't see it yet, so start guessing."
- Weekly Interrogation: "This week's suspects have had time to get their stories straight. Ask the question they haven't prepared for."
- Dungeon Quest: "The way in is open and nobody has told you what's down there. Say what you do first."
- Connect: "1. Copy the shop's link. 2. Paste it into your assistant's settings. 3. Approve the connection. Then tell it you want to play."
- Nothing played: "You haven't played anything yet. Pick one and see what it does when you talk back." Button: "Choose a game"
- Payment failed: "Your card was declined and nothing has been charged." Button: "Try again"
- Disconnect: "Disconnect your assistant? Your saved progress stays. It won't be able to start games." Buttons: "Disconnect", "Stay connected"
