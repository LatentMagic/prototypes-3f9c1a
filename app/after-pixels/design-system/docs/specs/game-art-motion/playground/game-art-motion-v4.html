Game art motion, v4

Roll call, refined. The whole card is the hover surface. The art moves into its hover pose and holds it until you leave. Going in and coming back use the same pace: 400ms on one curve. Going in, the shapes follow one another; coming back, they return together. Travel is capped in pixels, so large art moves no further than small art and finishes in the same time.

B

Roll call

Every shape swaps its form, one after another, in a slow wave. Daily Groups' squares round into dots. Casebook's bars round their tops into figures and each rises by a different amount, then the sun turns square and sinks slightly. In 36,000 Summers Ago the small teal square rounds, the sun turns square and rises behind the peak, the peak grows from its base, and the cream square rounds and hops.

On touch screens

  • The pose plays while a finger is down on the card, the way a button shows it's pressed, and returns when you lift.
  • Nothing is overridden. A tap opens the game, and a long press still brings up the phone's own menu (open in new tab, copy link, preview).
  • A scroll that starts on a card cancels the pose, so swiping past tiles stays quiet.

One description, any frame

Each piece is specified in words first. The words say what must stay true and what may move, so the art can be recomposed for square, landscape or wide slots without losing anything.

Daily Groups

The four groups of a solved board. Sixteen equal squares in a 4 by 4 grid on indigo, one colour per row: amber, teal, orange, lilac from the top.

Always
The grid is square and whole. Gaps are about a third of a square. At least one gap's width of indigo on every side.
May change
In wider frames the grid sizes to the height and stays centred; indigo fills the sides.

Casebook

A line-up under a low sun. Three upright bars stand on the bottom edge, orange, lilac and cream, with an amber sun above and to the right of them. Indigo ground.

Always
Bars run off the bottom edge. Lilac is tallest, cream shortest. The sun is a whole circle and never touches an edge or a bar.
May change
In wider frames the bars get narrower and closer together, and the sun moves further right and lower relative to the frame.

36,000 Summers Ago

Dawn over open ground. A lilac peak rises from the bottom edge, right of centre, with an amber sun half behind its left slope. A small teal square sits low on the left, a smaller cream square high on the right. Forest ground.

Always
The peak runs off the bottom edge. The sun is a whole circle, partly covered by the peak. The cream square is the smallest shape.
May change
In wider frames the peak narrows relative to the frame and moves towards the centre, with the sun kept tucked against its left slope.
