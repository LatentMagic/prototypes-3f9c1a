### 1. Existing Logos & Widely Used Icons

* **Google Material Design “Widgets” Icon**
  * **Description**: A 2x2 arrangement of geometric primitives—specifically three squares and one circle (or in some versions, a square, circle, triangle, and square)—used across Android and the web to denote widgets, modular add-ons, and mini-tools.
  * **Closeness**: **Near-identical** in silhouette, cadence, and concept.
  * **Confidence**: High (widely documented in Google Material Symbols).

* **Microsoft Corporate Mark & Windows Store**
  * **Description**: A 2x2 flat grid composed of four equal squares separated by a uniform white cross-gutter (red, green, blue, yellow).
  * **Closeness**: **Clearly similar**. The spatial rhythm, grid proportions, and gutter spacing match almost 1:1, differing only by substituting one square for a circle.
  * **Confidence**: 100% sure.

* **Stock SaaS / OS “App Switcher” & “Extensions” Glyphs** (e.g., Salesforce App Launcher, standard Chromium browser extension icons)
  * **Description**: 2x2 or 3x3 grids where three cells are uniform blocks/dots and the fourth is accented, hollowed, or replaced with a round badge to signal an active state or modular plugin.
  * **Closeness**: **Clearly similar**.
  * **Confidence**: High (omnipresent across enterprise web apps and browser toolbars).

* **Sony Interactive Entertainment (PlayStation Geometric Shapes)**
  * **Description**: The proprietary four-symbol mark (Triangle, Circle, Cross, Square), frequently locked up in a tight 2x2 grid on accessories, packaging, and digital UI.
  * **Closeness**: **Same family only** overall, but it directly deploys the same square-and-circle geometric language within interactive entertainment.
  * **Confidence**: 100% sure.

* **Tic-Tac-Toe / Connect Four Casual Game App Icons** (e.g., various iOS/Android indie game stores)
  * **Description**: Grids (2x2 or 3x3) where solid grid cells hold a contrasting round token to signify a game in progress.
  * **Closeness**: **Same family only**.
  * **Confidence**: High (generic trope across app store casual puzzle listings).

### 2. The Three Real Risks

1. **Google Material "Widgets" Icon (Fatal Distinctiveness Risk)**
   * **Arrangement**: A literal match: a 2x2 grid containing three squares and one circle.
   * **Colour**: While Material icons are often monochrome, replacing monochrome fill with flat teal/amber does not impart sufficient visual distinctiveness.
   * **Field of Business**: Consumer web shop & browser-playable games. The UK IPO (and global registries) will view a 2x2 grid mixing squares and a circle as a functional UI symbol denoting "widgets" or "add-ons," rejecting it under Section 3(1)(b) of the UK Trade Marks Act 1994 for lack of inherent distinctiveness.

2. **Microsoft Corporation (Opposition & Dilution Risk)**
   * **Arrangement**: The flat 2x2 grid with narrow cross-gutters is universally associated with Microsoft. Swapping one square for an equal-width circle looks like an intentional variant.
   * **Colour**: Distinct from Microsoft’s quad-colour palette, but insufficient to overcome the shared silhouette.
   * **Field of Business**: Identical overlap. Microsoft operates major game stores (Xbox, PC Game Pass), consumer web storefronts, and consumer AI assistants (Copilot). Microsoft’s legal team aggressively defends its 2x2 silhouette against software, gaming, and AI marks.

3. **Sony Interactive Entertainment (Gaming Trade Dress Risk)**
   * **Arrangement & Visuals**: Direct pairing of solid square primitives alongside a round counter.
   * **Field of Business**: Video games and digital gaming platforms (Class 9 and Class 41).
   * **Why**: Sony vigorously polices square-and-circle combinations in the games sector. Using the exact visual vocabulary of console gaming hardware for an indie game shop invites immediate C&D pressure from their brand-protection counsel.


### 3. Reading at 16px: Stock UI Icon vs. Brand Mark

**Yes, at 16px it overwhelmingly reads as a generic UI affordance or a system notification state rather than a proprietary brand.**

* **Rasterization & Silhouette Collapse:** At a 16×16 pixel canvas (with typical 1–2px margins), each cell receives roughly 4×4 pixels, separated by a 2px gutter. The subpixel anti-aliasing required to round off the corners of a 4px circle is barely perceptible; to the human eye, it reads simply as four blurred tiles or three tiles plus a rendering artefact.
* **Affordance Mimicry:** Rather than telling a brand story (*“three board spaces, one player piece”*), users instinctively parse a 2×2 grid with one contrasting top-right element as:
  1. An **“App Launcher / Bento Grid”** icon with one active tab selected.
  2. A **“Notification Alert”** where a teal system icon has an unread amber notification badge pinned to its top-right corner.
  3. A browser **“Widgets / Extensions”** toggle.
* **Verdict:** Because the grid geometry is completely rigid and symmetrical, it lacks the silhouette asymmetry and distinct macro-shape needed to achieve brand recall in a crowded browser tab.

---

### 4. Three Corrective Changes (One Line Each)

1. **Dynamic Placement (In-Flight Move):** Shift the amber circle slightly out of the grid—hovering just above or sliding into the top-right slot—to break the rigid 2×2 footprint and visibly signal an active player move.
2. **Scale & Overlap Contrast:** Enlarge the circle so it overlaps the adjacent teal squares (or projects past the outer grid boundary), transforming the image from a flat tile matrix into a physical game piece dropped onto a board.
3. **Isometric / Diamond Orientation:** Rotate the entire arrangement by 45 degrees into an isometric diamond, instantly divorcing the mark from flat desktop UI/Windows grid silhouettes while strongly evoking tabletop strategy boards.
