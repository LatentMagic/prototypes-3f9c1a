# Plan: Delve's session page, v2

Proposed from the owner's review of v1 (2026-10-28, brain dump). Nothing here is ratified.

## What the review said

**Leans (said, not ratified)**
- Die: Hex only. Stone (reads as a document icon) and Mark are out. Never Roll20's faceted face.
- No tooltip or legend: a die that needs explaining is a drawing fault.
- Liked in A: a column per room, linked selection, the line naming the held roll; on a phone, the tight moment tiles.
- Liked in B: the held moment drawn large beside the picture, so detail appears only for what you pick.
- Liked in C: the cleanest, and the furthest from Escape's lanes.
- Liked: the figures at the top right, to be thought of as a character sheet; gear on it if there is room.

**Problems**
- Result statement: the setting's sentence used as a title, one form in all four concepts, not ideated. It doesn't match "Escaped" / "Solved".
- The outcome is said three times: headline, "· Overrun", "Threat, full".
- Moment tiles state facts but carry no signal: the nat 20 doesn't read as the best roll.
- Die: the natural 1's crack is disliked. Two d20 (the second hex behind it) and the weak hit (amber outline) don't explain themselves.
- Room numbers in A and C aren't understood (order of first entry, a repeat means going back).
- The detail line: too far down on a phone, in a different place at each width, and hover changes it while you scroll to it. It gets out of hand with many rolls.
- A is close to Escape's lanes. B's stair is bloated. D has no X factor. C's letters A–D go. C's phone strip wraps badly. C leaves dead space on the right.
- Every concept shares the same moments and the same top: not enough range.

**Tension to resolve**
- A works best on a phone but is closest to Escape; C is the most distinct but fails on a phone.
- Hover preview was the owner's ask, and it is what makes the detail move under you.

## Proposed passes

Each pass is its own row in one v2 playground (`delve-v2.html`, copied from v1; v1 left as it is), reviewed before the next starts.

1. **Result statement and character sheet (the top of the bespoke part).** 3–4 ideated forms of the statement, each aligned with Escape's and Word's one-word results, with the setting's sentence kept but not as the title. One character sheet: hero, hit points, threat, rooms reached, gear, with the outcome said once. Shared across games, so it goes first.
2. **The die, Hex only.** One sheet of every state (strong, weak, miss, nat 20, nat 1, two d20) in 2–3 drawings, judged on whether each reads without a legend. Replaces the crack and the ghost hex.
3. **The picture.** Three new concepts, more divergent than v1, starting from C's distinctness and B's large held moment, with A as the yardstick. Each must:
   - give moments a signal (why it is the moment, not only its numbers);
   - make the room numbers self-evident, going back included;
   - put the held-roll detail next to the picture at every width, and decide hover: preview the highlight only, with the detail changing on press;
   - fill the width on desktop and hold on a phone with 8–9 stops.
4. **AA check** on every pass: contrast of dice, pips and muted text.

The hard play stays, and gains a sixth stop of going back to stress the phone.

## Cleanup, carried into every pass (owner, 2026-10-28: "I presume you would do that")
- The bar's Die row goes: Hex only. Stone, Mark and their code go.
- Why: shorter, one line per concept, no prose.
- Repeats cut: the outcome said once; "Roll N" only where the number helps.
- Alignment: figures, pips, tile edges and the detail line on one grid; the snags the owner saw in A, listed with him before fixing.
- Line breaks in the detail line set by design (break between facts, not mid-fact).

## Still open from v1
The moment rule; spoilers; achievements (the brief lists them, no concept shows them).
